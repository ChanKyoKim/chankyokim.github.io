Policy evaluation in a generated scene

VLA policy evaluation in generated 3D scenes, using controlled background comparisons in Isaac Sim. Real-robot validation is planned.

Toyota Research Institute, 2026 · AnyWorld × PolaRiS · food-bussing · Isaac Sim · DROID validation planned

PolaRiS evaluates manipulation policies inside a photographed real-to-sim scan of the workcell. We replace that scan with a Gaussian field that AnyWorld generated from a single frame, keep the robot, objects and evaluation protocol fixed, and measure how the background affects policy evaluation. The results below are preliminary simulation experiments; validation on the physical DROID station is planned.

What we did

Render the scan along an orbit anchored on the policy camera, keep one first frame, and let AnyWorld roll the scene forward for 161 frames. Depth Anything 3 depth over the generated frames accumulates into a 3DGS field of 4.6 M Gaussians, decimated 4× and converted into PolaRiS’s scene format. The evaluation observation is a three-layer composite — background splat, then the ray-traced robot arm and objects through a semantic mask — so everything that moves is pixel-identical across conditions and only the background changes. Then the full food-bussing benchmark: five VLA policies (two π0 checkpoints, π0.5, π0-FAST, a PaliGemma-based policy) × 3 backgrounds × 15 rollouts.

The observation stream the policy actually consumed, on our generated background: π0.5 (two episodes) and π0-FAST — approach, grasp, carry, bowl, next object; 2/2 objects placed.

What we found

The evaluation runs end to end on a background generated from one frame. Policies pick objects up and place them in the bowl (lift 37% vs 27% on the scan, bowl placement 15% vs 12%), and the policy ranking comes out the same as on the real scan. Then the harder finding: a deliberately 4×-blurred scan preserves the ranking just as well (r = 0.975), because the background is 96% of the pixels and 0% of the dynamics. A benchmark that cannot tell a generated scene from a blurred one is measuring the wrong thing.

Episode 0, frame 0 — the same robot and objects on three backgrounds: the PolaRiS scan, our generated field, and blur4, the scan degraded 4× as a control.

Why it matters

These experiments establish a pipeline for evaluating policies against generated visual backgrounds while holding the simulated robot and objects fixed. The blurred-background control motivates evaluations that also test dynamics, occlusion and memory. Transfer to physical-robot performance remains to be validated.

The baseline, same protocol

Same policies, same objects, same initial conditions on the original PolaRiS scan.

Generated input

The 161-frame AnyWorld generation (576×384) that is the only input to our field. Anchor-frame PSNR of the generated field against the scan is 11.2 dB (frames 0–49); both fields lose coverage once the orbit swings 96° wide, and the evaluation only ever uses the policy camera.

Setup

Food-bussing, 450 steps per episode; Isaac Sim 6.0.1 / Isaac Lab 6.1.14 on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Byte-comparing the two scene directories, the only differing file is the background splat.ply; initial conditions, splat placement and the resulting trajectories are identical.
