AnyWorld: A World Model That Builds Any World to Scale

Generate video, revisit a scene, and export a world in metric 3D — with one persistent memory.

Chankyo Kim, Vitor Guizilini, Maani Ghaffari · Toyota Research Institute · Under review at CVPR 2027

Generate a world, move through it in meters, and return to what you have seen. AnyWorld adds feed-forward 3D Gaussian memory to a Wan-based video backbone, connecting camera-controlled generation with a persistent, exportable 3D scene.

Condition on more of the world

One image starts the world. More views make it better informed.

Real photographs can enter the memory with their camera poses and depth, without retraining the model. These experiments test two sources of information: a later view along the route and the side cameras on the same vehicle.

Connect a command to a physical distance

Camera motion should mean the same thing in every world.

A plausible trajectory can still cover the wrong distance. AnyWorld uses a shared metric convention for camera poses, depth, and memory, and evaluates executed motion against sensor references without fitting its scale after generation.

Keep a world across time

Leave a place, then come back to it.

Accumulated keyframes bring previously observed structure back into view. Return-to-start trajectories compare the final generated view with the original image and isolate the effect of spatial retrieval.

Take the world beyond video

The keyframes used for generation also become a 3D asset.

Stored metric RGB-D keyframes decode into a Gaussian scene without reconstructing the generated video afterward. They can also be fused into a surface mesh. The export preserves the camera coordinate frame, making scale and placement testable against real sensor measurements.

In the paper's robot-lab evaluation, Gaussian assets achieve 2.8% median scale error and 3.1 cm placement error using camera extrinsics without scale fitting. This evaluation is separate from the three mesh examples above. AnyWorld × PolaRiS explores policy evaluation in generated backgrounds.

Evaluation scope

The 8% figure is the median of endpoint displacement errors across six domains. It measures camera-motion scale, not overall image quality or reconstruction accuracy. The 24–65% range refers to Lyra 2.0, GEN3C, and WorldStereo 2.0 with the same sensor anchor and without trajectory alignment.

The paper’s model uses six training datasets, 14,648 sequences, and 7.0 million frames. Later retraining and asset-refinement experiments are separate ongoing work; these videos do not imply that those models have been adopted.

Metric execution still depends on the input scale. Exported assets can contain holes, low-opacity surfaces, and appearance errors. PolaRiS footage is simulation-only; physical-robot policy validation remains planned.
