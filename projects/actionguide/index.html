ActionGuide: Learning Transferable Action-Grounded Reasoning for Closed-Loop Driving

A multimodal model that checks a frozen planner's trajectories and corrects them when none is safe — trained only in simulation, measured on real-world scenes and in closed loop.

Co-authored research · Submitted to ICLR 2027 · CARLA training data, real-world evaluation (nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse 2), closed-loop driving in AlpaSim

A multimodal model can describe a driving scene. Can it tell whether a specific trajectory is safe — and does that judgment make a car drive better? ActionGuide puts a multimodal model in the loop as an online guide to a frozen motion planner: it rejects unsafe proposals and supplies a correction when none is acceptable. Trained only on simulated data, it is the most accurate open model we evaluated on real-world action judgments, and it gives the frozen planner its largest closed-loop gain.

Result 1 · Closed loop

A better guide makes the same planner drive better.

The planner (DrivoR) and its proposals are held fixed; only the guide changes. Every guide is evaluated on the same 131 curated AlpaSim scenarios — traffic signals, unprotected turns, yielding, work zones — under the same verification-and-correction interface. Pretrained models already help a little. ActionGuide helps most: it lowers at-fault collisions and off-road departures together, rather than trading one for the other.

Result 2 · Sim-to-real

Behavior knowledge learned in simulation transfers to real scenes.

Every training example comes from CARLA; every evaluation question comes from real driving logs (nuScenes, Waymo Open Motion, Argoverse 2 — 2,539 questions). Fine-tuning raises overall accuracy from 41.4% to 53.5%: Consequence Prediction +23.7 points, Corrective Guidance +14.2, Evidence Grounding +9.6. The gains are largest where the judgment is about driving behavior — unnecessary stopping, collision risk, crosswalk entry. Where the judgment depends on exact geometry and camera calibration (wide turns, corner cutting), accuracy drops; we report that as the limit of what simulation teaches.

Result 3 · Why the benchmark matters

Action-grounded accuracy predicts closed-loop utility.

Across seven guides, overall accuracy on the four tasks correlates with the closed-loop at-fault scene score at Pearson r = 0.901 (Spearman ρ = 0.964). Evidence Grounding alone already correlates at r = 0.858. A driving VLA can be good at one task and still drive poorly: Qwen-Drive-1.0 has the best trajectory-verification accuracy (69.2%) but scores 35.2 in closed loop as a standalone planner. Breadth across the four capabilities is what tracks driving.

Result 4 · Reasoning before acting

Explicit reasoning makes pretrained knowledge actionable.

On 28 traffic-light and yielding scenarios, asking the pretrained guide to reason about signals and traversable space before recommending a behavior raised the at-fault scene score from 19.33 (planner alone) and 20.93 (proposal-by-proposal verification) to 31.24. That result is what motivated ActionGuide-Train: supervision that ties scene evidence, consequences and a concrete driving decision together.

How it is trained

A simulation data engine with verified labels

ActionGuide-Train is built in CARLA from scenarios drawn from the California Driver's Handbook. For each decision state it creates contrasting behaviors under the same observation — rule-based variations that comply or conflict, plus proposals mined from real planner rollouts — replays them, and labels all four tasks from simulator state. The guide is Cosmos-Reason2-8B, fine-tuned on this data for one epoch.

What the evidence supports: a guide trained only in simulation improves real-world action judgments and improves a fixed planner in closed-loop simulation. It does not make DrivoR the strongest planner — the standalone Alpamayo-1.5 scores 63.8 under the same protocol — and closed-loop results are AlpaSim simulation, not on-vehicle driving. Guidance can also hurt: the cases below include a regression and an execution failure.

Setup

Inputs to the guide: four front-view history frames over the preceding 2 s plus the current front, front-left and front-right views, with the candidate trajectory drawn as an overlay. Trajectories: eight waypoints at 0.5 s (4 s horizon). Corrective anchors: hard left, light left, straight, light right, hard right, stop. Training data: CARLA 0.9.16. Fine-tuning: Cosmos-Reason2-8B, LLM backbone updated with the vision encoder and aligner frozen, bfloat16 on 8 GPUs, one epoch, AdamW, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁵, effective batch 16. Real-world evaluation: 2,539 multiple-choice questions from the validation splits of nuScenes, Waymo Open Motion and Argoverse 2. Closed loop: AlpaSim, 131 human-curated decision-critical scenarios, scene score as the primary metric.
