Chankyo Peter Kim

Ph.D. Candidate in Robotics at the University of Michigan · Research Scientist Intern, World Foundation Model Team, Toyota Research Institute

Los Altos, CA · Ann Arbor, MI

I build world models with explicit 3D state for spatial reasoning and robot learning.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Robotics at the University of Michigan, advised by Prof. Maani Ghaffari. Since May 2026 I have been a Research Scientist Intern on the World Foundation Model team at Toyota Research Institute, building foundation models for driving and robotics. Before that I worked on factor-graph visual SLAM at Bear Robotics and co-founded OUTTA, an AI-education non-profit that has taught 3,000+ students.

My work runs from representation to control, and one rule holds at every level: the representation should transform lawfully as the world does. Reductive Lie Neurons (ICML 2026) extends equivariance to general linear symmetries on Lie algebras. E3DGS asks what a good latent for a 3D Gaussian looks like, and answers with one compact embedding that carries position, covariance and color together, so a single equivariant network can process a splat without Clebsch–Gordan machinery — and can be pre-trained with no labels at all, by masked prediction over the splats. GINIO (CoRL 2026) applies the same law to sensing: a learned inertial measurement — mean and covariance alike — survives remounting the IMU on a real robot without retraining.

At TRI, I lead AnyWorld, a camera-controlled world model with persistent metric 3D memory. It generates across driving, indoor scenes, and games, accepts additional real views without retraining, and exports its keyframe memory as a reusable 3D scene. Across six evaluation domains, its median endpoint displacement error is 8%, compared with 24–65% for the evaluated baselines under the same sensor anchor and without alignment. Explore the demonstrations or the research story. Related AnyWorld × PolaRiS work studies policy evaluation in generated scenes; physical DROID validation remains planned.

My work on control includes RL post-training of a driving policy and planning through a learned Gaussian world model for pushing. I also co-authored ActionGuide (submitted to ICLR 2027), which studies multimodal reasoning and post-training around a frozen driving planner. Across these projects, I connect representation and reasoning quality to measurable behavior through closed-loop evaluation.

Underneath the projects is one question: what makes a representation of the world good. My answer is three properties that reinforce one another. It is aligned — it carries the meaning it should, across the views and modalities it is drawn from. It is controllable — it factors the world into parts you can read off and intervene on, which is what turns perception into prediction and planning. And it is consistent — it transforms lawfully as the world does, so a new viewpoint or a later moment maps to something you can still trust. Geometric equivariance, in this picture, is one instance of consistency: a clean and powerful one, but a single member of a larger family. The closed loop is where I find out whether the other two hold.

If you work on world models, 3D/4D scene representations, or evaluating embodied agents, write to chankyo@umich.edu.

We show that low-degree spherical-harmonic color is algebraically isomorphic to a rank-2 geometric tensor, enabling a unified $\mathfrak{gl}(3)$ embedding for $SE(3)$-equivariant 3DGS — without Clebsch–Gordan tensor products.
