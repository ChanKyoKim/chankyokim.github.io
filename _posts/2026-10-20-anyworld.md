<p class="pj-lede">A world model should do more than produce a plausible next frame. For spatial reasoning and robotics, it should preserve a meaningful scale, remember places after leaving them, and leave behind a scene that another system can use.</p>

AnyWorld brings these requirements together through metric RGB-D keyframe memory and feed-forward 3D Gaussian rendering. The video generator builds on the Wan-based backbone of Lyra 2.0. The memory provides a common representation for camera control, additional observations, and metric scene export.

## A good-looking path can still have the wrong scale

A generated drive may appear to turn at the right places while traveling a different distance from the camera command. Aligning the recovered trajectory to the requested path can conceal that error. We instead compare executed displacement with sensor-based references, without a fitted scale or trajectory alignment.

The key diagnostic changes the depth used to initialize memory while keeping the first image and camera command fixed. Generated motion responds to the memory's scale. This motivates treating depth, poses, and generated state as one metric system.

With the same sensor anchor, AnyWorld reports **8% median endpoint displacement error across six domains**, compared with **24–65%** for the compared Lyra 2.0, GEN3C, and WorldStereo 2.0 models. Appearance, geometry, and scene persistence are evaluated separately.

## A memory that conditions a frame and exports a scene

The model stores observations as RGB-D keyframes with poses in a shared coordinate system. Selected keyframes are lifted into Gaussians and rendered from the requested camera to condition subsequent video chunks. Previously seen structure can therefore return to view as the camera moves.

The stored keyframes also feed the asset decoder, producing a metric Gaussian scene. This export reuses the generation memory instead of starting a new reconstruction from the finished video. The conditioning render and the final exported Gaussian field are different products of the same keyframe memory.

A surface mesh is another derivative: TSDF fusion combines those RGB-D keyframes into geometry. That provides a route toward simulator integration, but mesh export alone does not establish collision quality, correct physical parameters, or reliable control.

## More observations can enter the same memory

Real photographs with poses and depth can enter memory before generation, without an additional training run. We test two driving settings: inserting a later image along the route and adding the vehicle's side-camera images.

<div class="research-table" markdown="1">

| Memory input                            | Mean LPIPS ↓      | Improved scene–seed pairs |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| First frame → first frame + later image | 0.543 → **0.484** | 33/34                     |
| Front camera → front + side cameras     | 0.559 → **0.339** | 28/30                     |

</div>

The experiments keep the first image, command, and generation seed fixed. Images from another scene and images placed at incorrect poses provide controls. The highlighted videos on the [project page]({{ '/projects/anyworld/#extra-views' | relative_url }}) follow the experiment's coverage-based selection rule.

The extra views come from the same recording with calibrated poses and sensor depth. Separate visits, uncertain calibration, and changed lighting remain harder settings.

## Revisiting a place tests what the model remembers

A return-to-start trajectory supplies a concrete target: the original image. The model leaves the view, generates the intervening scene, and should recover previously observed structure on its return. Comparisons with spatial retrieval disabled help distinguish persistent memory from short-term frame continuity.

The [longer driving loops]({{ '/projects/anyworld/#persistent-memory' | relative_url }}) test the same idea across larger excursions. They do not establish indefinite persistence: appearance can drift, geometry can degrade, and performance varies across trajectory families.

## From a metric asset to robot evaluation

The paper's robot-lab evaluation places Gaussian scenes using camera extrinsics and compares them with sensor references. It reports **2.8% median scale error and 3.1 cm placement error**. Geometry can remain useful even where rendered appearance is incomplete.

[AnyWorld × PolaRiS]({{ '/projects/polaris-real2sim2real/' | relative_url }}) explores a separate question: can a generated background support policy evaluation? Its current results are simulation comparisons. Background quality and manipulation performance need separate evaluation; successful simulated rollouts do not substitute for physical-robot validation.

These distinctions shape the next step: improve the exported scene's appearance and coverage while preserving metric geometry, then measure how those changes affect downstream planning and control.
